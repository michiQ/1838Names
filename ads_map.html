Where the businesses were

Business ads on the street grid

113 newspaper ads placed at 85 addresses. Tap an icon to read the ad. Drag to move the map; pinch, scroll or use the buttons to zoom. The streets are drawn to scale and each icon sits where that house number stands. Where businesses stood close together, their icons are lined up along the street in house-number order, with a thin line back to the true spot; zoom in to see them settle into place.

How the icons are placed. The streets are drawn to scale from today's Philadelphia address map. Philadelphia renumbered its houses in 1857 and those numbers still stand, so an ad from 1857 on is placed at the spot that house number has today (913 Lombard is where 913 Lombard is now). Earlier ads carry old house numbers and are placed only from a cross street named in the ad. An icon with a dashed ring is an approximate spot: tap it to see why. Streets are named as the ads name them; where a name has changed, today's name follows in brackets.

Street lines and address points: a present-day Philadelphia address-point file (OpenAddresses layout).
