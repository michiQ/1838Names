THE RESPIRATOR.
Mr, Combe has exhibited in New York an in-
genious contrivance called a Respirator, invented
in England to avoid the irritating effect of cold
air upon the lungs, in asthmatic and consump-
tive patients. The: Respirator is worn over the
mouth, and is constructed on strictly scietitific
.principles. It consists of the instrdment proper
and its appendages, ‘The instrument is formed
by a number of wire plates or sieves made some-
what after the manner of those of Davy’s aafety
lamp, and fixed in a flexed frame The breath, in
passing through these plates, yields ite caloric,
which is taken up by the cold air which is drawn
through the instrument during respiration, thus
raising it from the freezing point to from 60 to 80
degiees, according.to the quality of the Respirator,
used. The air, too, in its passage inward, parts
with the grosser irritating particles which it may
contain. The Respirator is recommended by
some of the first medical men in England. Mr.
Combe says that a friend of his, who had for some
time coughed so during the night as to be hardly
able to sleep, was enabled. to do so without dis-
turbance on the first night of wearing the instru-
ment, and until he recovered.
