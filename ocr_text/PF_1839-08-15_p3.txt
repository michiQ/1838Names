“ANTESLAVERY ALMANAC, FOR 1840.
‘A supply of this litle anti-slavery “annual”
has been received and is now ready for sale at the
“office of the Freeman, 72 North Seventh street.
Its contents indicate a good deal of industry, and
the ‘‘cuts” are unusually apt and striking. Many
of them furnish a “text” of such stern reality that}

no illustration by any “commentary” is needed.
Each is a sermon of itself. But some minds may
be disposed to shrink from the supposed “exagge-
ration” of these “pictorials.” They will be deem-
edharsh and incendiary. The severity, however,
will be found to consist in the great fidelity with
“ which real scenes are represented. ‘The “cut”
which represents a man escaping from a slave
state, with the chains falling from him; and his
being caught and re-manacled in a free state, is
admirably conceived. It tells a powerful truth—
a truth which ought to be felt as a very thorn in
every bosom in the nation. It. will be so felt.
Many a smitten heart will writhe in its presence.
‘Tt will fasten itself there, “with hooks of steel,”
‘and torture into conviction even the callous and
apparently obdurate. “What have we to do
with slavery!” Let this one simple truth-telling
picture give its silent response. No man of sense
can look it in the face, and repeat that stupid
question. It would instantly rebuke the grum-
bling dough-face. It would strike him dumb.
No ‘matter, therefore, how many people get a
~sight of it. It ought to be on every door post at
~ the North. There aro many other good views;
but we cannot enumerate them. Buy the alma-
nac and look at them. Scatter them abroad over
the land. ‘They will “answer” for all meredians,
“wherever the foot-print of the slave is seen—whie-
ther onthe southern sands, or in: the great “Ap-
pian way” to Canada freedom, Yes, pave that
“sway” with them—and ever other, where the
kidnapper and the slave-catcher tread like: the
"prowling wolf. Fling them out all over the time-
serving, contaminated North, until they’ shall
‘epring up like dragons’ teeth planted in our soil.
‘Possibly a single “fip” may.release a slave!—or
it may light hina on to liberty! Many‘ stern
heart has been conquered by an humble instru-
ment, It may beso again. Give the little “an.
nual” then, a thorough scattering—let not a soli-
tary one slumber through the year.
