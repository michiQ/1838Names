Of the Executive Committee of the Eastern
District,

In presenting to the Society a brief his-
tory of their proceedings, during the past six
months, the Committee cannot but congratu-
late their fellow members upon the rapid and
gratifying progress of the -righteous princi-
ples of liberty throughout the Eastern Section
of our state, and on the favorable position of
our concerns.at the present time, Although
some of the scenes through which abolitionists
in this section of oureountry,have been obliged
to pass, during the past year, have been terrific
and heart-rending; althongh they have wit-
nessed the fierce: passions of the multitude
mphing over all opposition, and substi-
tuting. their own will for the salutary opera-
tion of the laws; although the torch of the
incendiary has been wantonly applied to their
property, and their persons have been threat-
ened with violence ; yet amidst all, the God
of Truth and Right has reigned triumphant,
calming the,tumultuous sea of evil passions,
and nerving with increased energy the perse-
cuted advocates of his anchangable Truth, to
new and increased exertions.

Since the last report of the Committee,
their attention has been directed to the re-
covery of damages for injuries sustained by
the Society in the burning of Pennsylvania
Hall. By that catastrophe a large number
of our valuable publications, which had just
been deposited in the new office, were de-
stroyed, and. it became necessary to apply $0
the County for an indemnity,

A Committee of six individuals were ap-
pointed, by the Judge of the Court, before
whom the facts were presented, and the case
argued by counsel. After numerous meetings,
the Committee granted an award of thirteen
hundred and seventy-five dollars. Anaward
of nine hundred dollars was granted by the
same Committee to our worthy collaborator,
Benj. Lundy, for injury sustained at the
same time. .
The Committee appointed in the ease of
the Pennsylvania Hall, have not yet dis-
charged the duty of their appointment. Some
resigned, and others, from indisposition and
other causes, have failed to come together at
the times agreed on; so that till within the
past week no full meeting has been held, and
consequently no award -has yet heen made.
Allarge number of respectable and industrious
mechanics, as well as the numerous body of
stockholders, whose standing as upright anid
useful citizens is not questioned, even. by
their opponents, have thus been subject to a
tedious delay of the satisfaction of their just
claims.

Daring the past fall and winter, several
agents have been actively employed in the
service of the Society, disseminating our
principles with success in various parts of
the country.
C. C. Burleigh and Wm. M. Chase, have
held numerous meetings in the counties of
Chester, Montgomery, Philadelphia, Bucks,
and Delaware ; sometimes joining in debate’
with opposers, and on other occasions ad-
dressing the people without opposition.
These meetings have generally”been svell at-
tended; being conducted without disturbances,
anil attended with very happy effects.
James Fulton, Jr., a zealous and intelli-
gent abolitionist of Chester County, bas
freely given his services to the Committée as
local agent, in which capacity he has lectured.
repeatedly and successfully in the counties
of Chester and Lancaster, and still continues
his labors as opportunity occurs.

C.F. Stollmeyer, an influential and able
German Editor, whose active participation in
our cause, since the burning of the Pennsyl-
vania Hall is generally known, has manifest-
ed a deep interest in the promulgation of our
sentiments amongst the German population,
and'has rendered important aid, in his labors
‘amongst this class, more particularly in the
counties of Bucks and Lancaster. ‘

One of the most important events which
have occurred since our last report, was the
holding of a Convention of Abolitionists for
Northern ‘Pennsylvania, at Montrose. Our
friends in this region had not, previous to
this convention, concentrated their efforts in
an organized form, and in order the more ef-
fectually to accomplish this object, they ob-
tained, by application to the Committee, the
aid of our friends, Wm. M. Chase and Charles
C. Burleigh. The former was busily occupied
for several weeks previous to the meeting, in
exciting an interestin the objects of the Con-
vention, in the neighboring country, and was
joined by the latter at the meeting, .

_This Convention was composed of about
400 members, and its proceedings were ehar-
acterized by a zeal and spirit worthy of the
important objects for which it was convened.’
